# Open questions (resolve before the kit is used for real decisions)

The kit uses the National Capital Commission (NCC), a federal Crown corporation, as its **worked example**, because its procurement data is public. Nothing in this repository states that any person works for, with or on behalf of it. Replace the example with your own organization's instruments.

1. **Which instrument governs a Crown corporation's procurement?**
   The TBS Directive on the Management of Procurement applies to departments in FAA Schedules I, I.1 and II (section 6.3); it does not name Crown corporations. Confirm with the organization's procurement or legal contact whether it is bound by the Directive, follows it by policy, or is governed by its own procurement policy. The kit treats TBS text as a *reference* until this is settled.
2. **The organization's own procurement policy and delegation of authority**: obtain the published text (or a version you are permitted to use) and add it to `sources/register.csv`. For the worked example, NCC's own published procurement policy has not been located or read.
3. **Government Contracts Regulations (SOR/87-402)**: referenced by Directive 4.10.1.4 for non-competitive justification. Fetch the exact exceptions text and register it.
4. ~~**"Fair and reasonable" wording**~~ — resolved 2026-09-22: this is the kit's own vernacular for the principles the register already verifies (best value, `TBS-DMP-3.1`; fairness, openness and transparency, `TBS-DMP-4.3.1`), not an official term needing its own citation. Contracting Policy Notice 2007-4 (`TBS-CPN-2007-4`) does use the exact phrase and is not rescinded, but its parent "Contracting Policy" was replaced by the current Directive in 2021, and the Directive's own text never uses the phrase. The template cites the verified principles, not this phrase itself, as the requirement.
5. **Trade agreements** (CFTA, CETA, CUSMA, WTO-GPA): thresholds and limited-tendering grounds that may apply. Not yet researched.
6. **Data handling**: is AI assistance permitted on the work, and which data may enter the tool (pricing, vendor names)? Confirm before loading real data.
7. **AI tool authorization** (blocks real-document ingestion). Planning assumption (register `ASSUMPTION-PLANNING`, not verified): Copilot can read PDFs, and is capable of and permitted to handle the documents. Still to record: which Copilot product; the highest security category it is approved for and by whom (access to raw files by IT staff is not that approval); whether prompts and uploads are retained or used for training; page and size limits; whether it can save files. The TBS guide (register `TBS-GENAI-GUIDE`) says to seek the chief security officer's approval before generative AI is used for protected information. `ingestion/COPILOT-MODE.md` lists these as its confirmations.
8. **Security categorization**: planning assumption (register `ASSUMPTION-CROWN-SECCAT`, not verified): a Crown corporation like the worked example applies the Treasury Board categories. Still open: what such an organization actually marks on construction documents, and whether unmarked commercial pricing is treated as Protected B (competitive advantage). Also read Appendix F (Security in Contracts) and the PSPC Contract Security Program / SRCL material, not yet done.
9. **Document-type definitions**: CCN, CO, CI, CD, SI and EA are written as working definitions (`ingestion/doc-types/relationships.md`). Check them against the general conditions of the contracts in use, and against real samples once sanitized.
10. **MasterFormat edition**: resolved by design. It is project dependent, so the chunker reads division names and edition from each project's `kb/masterformat.json`, built from that spec's own table of contents, and keeps section numbers as written. Nothing to decide centrally.
11. **OCR**: none is included. Scanned drawings and specs need an OCR route that the organization permits; decide before ingesting scans (the trial found two one-page survey sheets and some report pages with no text layer).
12. **Python or PowerShell on the target computer**: assumed unavailable. The gate, approval, chunking and index are designed as Power Automate flows (`ingestion/POWER-AUTOMATE.md`), which cannot do pattern matching (no regex). See #13.
13. **Power Automate prerequisites and unverified points**: assumed that flows with SharePoint and Outlook connectors can be created; Teams is not used and Office Scripts are not wanted. Still open: is there a private SharePoint site for the `Ingestion` library and the `Mapping` and `IngestLog` lists, and does a data-loss-prevention policy limit combining SharePoint and Outlook? Unverified in Power Automate (nothing has been run there yet; the flows were validated only against a Python reference): does *Get file content* return `.md` as text, does *Copy file* offer overwrite, does *Get files (properties only)* accept the filter used for regeneration, do secured outputs propagate as described, and does *Send email with options* behave as described (Approve/Reject, `SelectedOption`, 30-day wait)?
14. **Which access-control mechanism a real shared webapp deployment should use.** `webapp/auth.py` implements a per-caller token roster now (`ARCHITECTURE.md` calls this "Option A" — adequate for a small, known set of callers). A hosted-identity mechanism (Cloudflare Access or similar) was raised as the likely long-term answer but not decided; `resolve_caller()` is deliberately the only place that would need to change. Also undecided: where a shared instance would actually run, and whether/how `fair-price-corpus` gets a real query path (the "dual-bot" idea only becomes buildable once that exists — see `ARCHITECTURE.md`).
